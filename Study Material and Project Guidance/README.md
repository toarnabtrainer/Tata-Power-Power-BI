# Power_BI_Tutorial
Tutorial on MS-PowerBI

## Chapter Details
Here’s the **chapter list** with **learner-centric short summaries** for each.

---

### ✅ Chapter 1: Introducing Power BI

You get a big-picture view of what Power BI is and how its main building blocks fit together: datasets, reports, dashboards, and tiles. You’ll learn how to upload data (like Excel files), build your first simple report, pin visuals to a dashboard, use natural language (Q&A) to ask questions about your data, and try Quick Insights. By the end, you should be comfortable moving around the Power BI Service and creating basic visual analyses.

---

### ✅ Chapter 2: Sharing the dashboard

This chapter shows you how to share your work with others. You’ll learn how to share dashboards with colleagues inside (and in some cases outside) your organization, understand the idea of workspaces (personal vs group), and see how OneDrive for Business can keep your data up to date. You’ll also get a taste of viewing your dashboards on mobile devices, so you can access insights on the go.

---

### ✅ Chapter 3: Understanding data refresh

Here you dig into how Power BI keeps your data current. You’ll understand the difference between refreshing in Power BI Desktop vs in the Power BI Service, how imported data gets updated, and when a gateway is needed to reach on-premises data. You’ll practice publishing a Power BI Desktop file (PBIX) to the service and configuring scheduled refresh, so your dashboards don’t go stale.

---

### ✅ Chapter 4: Using Power BI Desktop

This chapter is all about building solid reports using **Power BI Desktop**. You’ll connect to different data sources (such as Excel and databases), clean and shape data in the Query Editor (filtering, splitting, merging, changing types), and load it into your model. You’ll also learn how to hide unnecessary tables/columns and handle things like correct month sorting, so your visuals behave the way you expect.

---

### ✅ Chapter 5: Getting data from services and content packs

You learn how to jump-start your analytics by using ready-made “packs” instead of starting from scratch. The chapter explains how to connect to online services via content packs, create custom datasets from these services, and use organizational content packs designed by others in your company. You’ll see how these packs provide prebuilt dashboards and reports, and how updates to them flow through to you.

---

### ✅ Chapter 6: Building a data model

Here you go deeper into the **data model** behind your reports. You’ll practice loading individual tables, defining relationships, and creating DAX measures and calculated columns. The chapter shows how well-designed measures can make your reports more powerful and flexible, and walks through a scenario where budget data is integrated and reallocated. You’ll come away understanding why modeling is crucial for serious Power BI work.

---

### ✅ Chapter 7: Improving Power BI reports

This chapter focuses on making your reports clearer, more insightful, and more professional. You’ll learn how to choose the right visuals for different questions (trend, comparison, distribution, etc.), when to pick one standard visual over another, and how to bring in custom visuals when needed. You’ll also see how DAX can help you build smarter metrics and how to handle high-density reports without overwhelming your users.

---

### ✅ Chapter 8: Using Microsoft Power BI in your company

Finally, you move from individual usage to **organization-wide** scenarios. You’ll explore how to connect Power BI to existing systems (on-premises and cloud databases, Analysis Services), understand the difference between data refresh and live connections, and see how Power BI integrates with Excel and other Office tools. The chapter also introduces security concepts like row-level security and shows how Power BI can be extended via APIs, real-time dashboards, embedding, and custom visuals.
